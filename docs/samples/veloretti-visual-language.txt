03  MISSION

"Every short car trip is an opening."

Replace short car trips in European cities with rides people actually prefer.

Not "reduce emissions". Not "reimagine mobility". A bike that is better than
the car for the trip someone is about to take, and available in the city where
they are taking it.
<<<PAGE>>>
04  POSITIONING

Electric cargo and commuter bikes engineered for daily distance, sold and
serviced through independent dealers.

Daily distance is the test. Everything on the bike is judged by whether it
survives a commute repeated five days a week for five years: the belt, the
bearings, the finish on the rack, the way the battery seats.

"We do not sell direct. The dealer is part of the product."

The dealer fits the bike, services it, and handles the warranty. That is not a
distribution choice, it is part of what is being sold, and it recurs through
the channel section as a constraint on everything we say.
<<<PAGE>>>
05  VISION

A European city where the fastest way across town is also the quietest.

We want the bike lane to be the default lane. That is a vision, not a mission:
it says where we are going, not what we do on Monday.
<<<PAGE>>>
06  THE PRODUCT WORLD

Three lines. Electric, City, Kids.

Electric carries the range and the assist. City carries the carrier, the
basket and the everyday trip. Kids carries the handover: the first bike a
parent buys that is built like the one they ride themselves.
<<<PAGE>>>
07  PHOTOGRAPHY

Daylight. Real streets. The bike in use rather than the bike on a plinth.

People are shown mid-trip, not posed beside the product. If a rider looks like
they are modelling, the shot is wrong.
<<<PAGE>>>
08  THE BIKE IN THE CITY

Context is the argument. A bike photographed against a white wall says nothing
about a commute. A bike photographed on the route says all of it.
<<<PAGE>>>
09  MARKETS

"Core four: NL, DE, BE, DK."

Netherlands, Germany, Belgium and Denmark are the served markets. Dealer
coverage, service network and warranty handling are in place in all four.

Austria and Switzerland appear on the map in a lighter tint, labelled
"2027 pipeline". They are not markets yet and nothing may be addressed to
them.
<<<PAGE>>>
10  DEALER NETWORK

340 partner dealers across the core four. The dealer is the service promise,
so any claim about availability or speed of service is a claim about them.
<<<PAGE>>>
11  AUDIENCES

"Meet Joris, Anna, Sven, Mila and Bram."

Joris rides 14 km each way and wants to arrive without changing his shirt.
Anna runs a fleet of twelve and cares about uptime.
Sven owns a shop and sells what he can service.
Mila does the school run and the groceries on the same bike.
Bram is buying his daughter her first real bike.

Five personas, three commercial audiences. There is no formal segment list in
this book, and that is deliberate: the personas are for writing, the segments
are for buying media.

12  WHO WE ARE NOT FOR

Sport. Racing. Weekend distance. We do not build for the ride that is the
point of the day.
<<<PAGE>>>
13  TONE IN ONE LINE

Say what the thing does, then stop.

14  HOW WE WRITE

"Say the number. Skip the adjective."

Fourteen approved headlines:

  90 km, then a socket.
  Built for the commute you already make.
  The rack is rated 27 kg. So is the one on the Pro.
  Arrive dry.
  Twelve years on the frame.
  Your dealer fits it. Your dealer fixes it.
  Belt driven, so nothing to oil.
  Two sizes. Both fit a child seat.
  The battery takes four hours.
  Lights that stay on at the lights.
  One bike, five days a week, five years.
  A bike that takes the shopping.
  Made to be serviced.
  Quiet, because there is nothing to rattle.

Do and do not:

  Do say      90 km range at 25 km/h assist
  Do not say  incredible range

  Do say      12-year frame warranty
  Do not say  built to last forever

  Do say      service within 48 hours
  Do not say  service you can count on

15  READING LEVEL

"Language a first-time buyer understands."

Someone buying their first electric bike should not have to know what a torque
sensor is to follow the sentence. Technical specification copy in the appendix
reads harder than this, and that is allowed there and nowhere else.
<<<PAGE>>>
16  NEVER WRITE

"No revolutions. We make bikes."

  revolutionary
  effortless
  game-changing
  unleash

Four further entries in this column are style notes rather than words: avoid
stacked adjectives, avoid the word "simply", do not open on a question, and do
not end on an ellipsis.
<<<PAGE>>>
17  NAMING

Product names are set in sentence case except where the model name is a proper
noun. Ace Two Pro, Ivy Two Lite, Caferacer.
<<<PAGE>>>
18  THE WORDMARK

Never redrawn, never recoloured, never set in another typeface. Black on
light, white on dark, and nothing else.
<<<PAGE>>>
19  LOGO CLEARANCE

Clear space around the wordmark is the height of the V on all four sides.
<<<PAGE>>>
20  CO-BRANDING

Dealer marks sit beside ours at equal optical weight, never inside our clear
space.
